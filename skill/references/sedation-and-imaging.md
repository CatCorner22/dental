# Sedation, anesthesia, and imaging documentation

## Governing rule order

Apply the strictest applicable requirement among:

1. state dental-practice act, board rule, permit, and facility rule
2. current federal requirement
3. current ADA guideline
4. current AAP and AAPD pediatric guideline when the patient is pediatric or has comparable developmental needs
5. current AAOMS guidance when oral and maxillofacial surgery practice is involved
6. manufacturer instructions and emergency protocol

Do not infer compliance from a completed template.

## Sedation depth

Use the clinician's documented depth:

- minimal sedation
- moderate sedation
- deep sedation
- general anesthesia

Sedation depth is independent of route. A patient may move to a deeper level than intended. The team must be able to rescue a patient from at least the next deeper level as required by the governing standard.

## Time-oriented anesthesia record

The narrative note never replaces the time-oriented anesthesia record. The source record must preserve actual times and required intervals. Record only verified facts.

### Preprocedure

- clinician fitness or candidacy determination
- intended procedure and intended depth
- responsible anesthesia provider and team roles
- permit, credential, and equipment check under local policy
- interval medical history and dental history review
- allergies, current medications, prior anesthesia or sedation experience, and relevant adverse events
- ASA physical-status classification assigned by the clinician
- focused physical examination and airway assessment
- height, weight, body mass index when required, age group, and baseline level of consciousness
- baseline blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, and other required measures
- fasting status, last intake by category and time, and clinician decision under the applicable protocol
- pregnancy status when clinically relevant and lawfully obtained
- escort or responsible adult plan when required
- consent discussion completed before cognition-altering medication
- day-of consent reaffirmation and opportunity for questions
- emergency drugs, oxygen, suction, defibrillation, ventilation, rescue equipment, and reversal agents checked under policy
- procedural pause or time-out

Do not hardcode fasting intervals into a patient note. Current ADA guidance includes category-based intervals for healthy patients having elective care, but delayed gastric emptying, GLP-1 receptor agonists, comorbidity, urgent care, age, and local rules may change the plan.

### Medication event

For every local anesthetic, sedative, analgesic, adjunct, reversal agent, oxygen or gas-mixture change, and emergency drug, record:

- exact generic name
- concentration when applicable
- amount and unit
- dose and unit, verified by the clinician
- route
- actual administration time
- administrator role
- reason when not self-evident
- response or adverse event

Never calculate, recommend, or infer a dose. Reconcile the narrative total with the time-oriented record.

### Intraoperative monitoring

Record required values at the actual times set by the applicable rule and clinical protocol. Do not invent evenly spaced times.

Potential fields include:

- level of consciousness and responsiveness
- oxygen saturation
- ventilation and chest excursion
- end-tidal carbon dioxide and waveform
- respiratory rate
- blood pressure
- heart rate and pulse
- electrocardiogram
- inspired or expired gases when applicable
- temperature when indicated
- airway interventions, stimulation, repositioning, suction, supplemental oxygen, assisted ventilation, rescue, or reversal
- material deviation, artifact, monitor failure, and reason a required monitor was precluded or invalid
- team-role change or handoff at the actual time
- positioning, padding, extremity protection, and eye protection when required for deep sedation or general anesthesia

The current ADA guideline expects continuous oxygenation monitoring and ventilation assessment for moderate sedation, including end-tidal carbon dioxide unless precluded or invalid, plus continual cardiovascular monitoring. Deep sedation and general anesthesia require the broader monitoring and staffing specified in the guideline. State rules may be stricter.

### Recovery and discharge

- medication and procedure end times
- sedation effectiveness and whether planned dental care was completed; if not, what stopped or remained
- transfer to recovery time and receiving staff role
- recovery position, airway status, oxygenation, ventilation, circulation, and level of consciousness
- serial recovery values at required times
- nausea, vomiting, pain, bleeding, adverse event, intervention, and response
- discharge criteria used and each criterion met
- clinician determination of satisfactory consciousness, oxygenation, ventilation, and circulation
- discharge time and destination
- responsible adult or escort verified in the clinical system
- written and verbal instructions supplied
- emergency contact method supplied in the clinical system
- extended observation after reversal when required
- clinician signature and required attestations
- postoperative contact status and owner of unresolved event follow-up

Do not write discharged stable without the supporting criteria.

## Pediatric controls

When pediatric guidance applies, also document:

- age- and size-appropriate equipment check
- presedation evaluation and focused airway examination
- fasting or urgent-procedure risk decision
- personnel able to rescue the child at the likely deeper level
- continuous observation and physiologic monitoring required for the depth
- recovery to the defined presedation level or approved discharge baseline
- parent or guardian instructions and legal authority only in the clinical system

## Imaging catalog

Use the universal imaging record for each acquired, ordered, received, or reviewed study.

### Ionizing, two-dimensional dental imaging

- periapical radiograph
- horizontal bitewing radiograph
- vertical bitewing radiograph
- occlusal radiograph
- full-mouth intraoral radiographic series
- panoramic radiograph
- lateral cephalometric radiograph
- posteroanterior cephalometric radiograph
- submentovertex, transcranial, transpharyngeal, or other named extraoral projection
- conventional tomography when applicable

### Ionizing, three-dimensional or medical imaging

- cone-beam computed tomography
- multidetector or medical computed tomography received from an outside source
- nuclear medicine, PET, SPECT, or fused study received from an outside source

### Non-ionizing imaging and digital records

- intraoral visible-light photograph
- extraoral visible-light photograph
- fluorescence, transillumination, or other optical caries image
- intraoral optical scan
- digitized study model
- three-dimensional facial surface scan
- magnetic resonance imaging received from an outside source
- ultrasonography received from an outside source
- other modality, named exactly

This catalog covers standard U.S. dental and oral-maxillofacial office records. Use other modality for a new or uncommon study rather than forcing a wrong label.

## Image safety controls

- Do not upload or analyze patient images with this Skill.
- Do not infer findings from image labels or procedure names.
- Do not call ionizing imaging routine.
- Record selection and interpretation separately.
- Record quality and limitation before impression.
- Link each unexpected finding to an action, referral, or reason no action was needed.
- Apply current radiation-safety and state equipment rules; do not copy outdated fixed schedules.
