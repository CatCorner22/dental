## Practice authority and responsibility

- Tenn. Code Ann. Title 63, Chapter 5 governs dentist, hygienist, and assistant licensure; the practice of dentistry; ownership; delegation; prescribing; specialties; and discipline.
- A person must hold the Tennessee authority required for the act performed. Teledentistry does not remove Tennessee licensure or supervision requirements.
- The licensed dentist remains responsible for acts performed under the dentist's supervision and may face discipline for allowing an auxiliary to perform an unauthorized act.
- Dental-practice ownership is regulated by Tenn. Code Ann. § 63-5-121, subject to statutory exceptions. Obtain counsel before changing ownership, management, or control rights.
- False, materially incorrect, or inconsistent patient-record entries and unauthorized disclosure of identifiable facts are listed as unprofessional conduct in Rule 0460-01-.12.

## Dental records

Rule 0460-02-.12 applies to office and other nonhospital dental records within its stated scope. Hospital records follow the separate hospital-record framework.

### Creation and minimum content

- Create and maintain a record for every patient for whom the dentist or a licensed, registered, or supervised team member performs a service or provides a professional consultation.
- Include the information required by Tenn. Code Ann. § 63-2-101(c), plus enough detail for a later dentist to identify the basis for the diagnosis, treatment plan, outcome, and continuity of care.
- At minimum, include tooth-condition charting; a concise description and treatment date for services; a concise medical history; dates, types, and amounts of pharmaceuticals prescribed or dispensed; and readable radiographs when required for the services.
- Treat radiographs and radiographic interpretations as part of the dental record.
- Record every pharmaceutical prescribed, dispensed, or distributed as required by Rule 0460-02-.11.

### Access, transfer, retention, and destruction

- Do not withhold records because of unpaid dental fees.
- Tenn. Code Ann. § 63-2-101 generally requires a copy or summary after a proper written patient or authorized-representative request within ten working days. Verify the current statute, authorization, format, and fee provisions before release.
- Retain adult dental records at least seven years after the last professional contact by the dentist or supervisee.
- Retain a minor's record at least one year after the patient reaches majority, or **ten** years after the last professional contact, whichever is longer. **Two Tennessee authorities state a different floor here and the practice should take the longer one.** Board Rule 0460-02-.12 gives seven years; the Tennessee Department of Health Standards of Practice Manual gives ten years from the last date of service. The adult rule above is not affected — three sources agree it is seven years — so the discrepancy is specific to minors. Ask counsel which governs this practice; until then, ten satisfies either reading and seven does not.
- Retain records for a patient described by the rule as incompetent indefinitely. Ask counsel to map this older term to current capacity and guardianship law.
- Do not destroy a record while the services are disputed.
- Destroy records only under a consistent office procedure that protects confidentiality. Keep a destruction log with enough group-level detail to identify whether a patient's record was included.
- Rules for retirement, death, departure, sale, and abandonment require custody, patient access, and notices. Build a written closure and succession plan.

### AI control

The Tennessee record must contain exact treatment dates and clinician identities. Enter those facts only in the EDR. Do not send them to the AI platform. Merge the de-identified draft into the correct chart and complete the required fields locally.

## Patient rights and consent

Rule 0460-01-.16 gives each patient rights that include dignity, treatment privacy, record confidentiality, information about diagnosis and options, participation in decisions, refusal with consequences explained, record access, pain assessment and management, and freedom from abuse.

- Document the diagnosis or evaluation, options, material risks, expected benefits, alternatives, no-treatment option, patient decision, and consequences of refusal when applicable.
- Do not use a form as a substitute for the discussion.
- Follow current Tennessee law for decision-making authority, minors, emergencies, incapacity, and mandatory reporting.

## Anesthesia and sedation

Rule 0460-02-.07 is the central state rule. The achieved or reasonably expected consciousness level controls the rule category, not the route.

### Permits and facility

- Nitrous oxide inhalation analgesia and qualifying antianxiety premedication do not require the state sedation permits described in the rule, but each remains subject to its operating requirements.
- Conscious sedation requires an individual limited or comprehensive permit. Parenteral conscious sedation, including IV moderate sedation, requires the comprehensive permit described by the rule.
- Deep sedation and general anesthesia require the individual deep-sedation or general-anesthesia permit.
- Sedation or anesthesia at an office also requires a separate dental-facility permit when Rule 0460-02-.07 applies. The compiled rule states a five-year facility-permit cycle and inspection framework.
- The current Board page states that all antianxiety premedication and sedation techniques other than nitrous oxide and oxygen for children age thirteen or younger require a comprehensive conscious-sedation permit.

### Team and equipment

- Conscious sedation requires at least one person in addition to the operating dentist.
- Deep sedation or general anesthesia requires at least two people in addition to the operating dentist.
- Team members must hold the required license or registration for assigned clinical acts and current resuscitation credentials required by the rule.
- Inspect anesthesia and sedation equipment on each day of use and keep the required log.
- Maintain required oxygen, positive-pressure delivery, suction, monitoring, recovery, emergency, backup-lighting, and age-appropriate equipment.
- Keep written emergency protocols with the emergency drugs and equipment. Train the team and document periodic training.

### Record and monitoring

For conscious sedation, the dental record must include medical history, current medications, allergies, consent for the anesthesia type, baseline blood pressure and pulse or a documented reason they could not be obtained, a time-oriented anesthesia record with drugs and doses, complications or morbidity, and discharge status. The rule requires continuous clinical observation, interval blood pressure and pulse, continuous pulse oximetry, trained recovery monitoring, and documentation of a prevented monitor.

For deep sedation or general anesthesia, add baseline temperature, interval blood pressure and pulse in the time-oriented record, continuous electrocardiography, continuous pulse oximetry, end-tidal carbon dioxide unless precluded or invalid, and temperature when a malignant-hyperthermia-triggering agent is used. Apply the current ADA monitoring guideline when it is stricter or more specific.

### Recovery, discharge, and reporting

- The office must use specific adult and pediatric discharge criteria.
- The record must support adequate ventilation, circulation, appropriate responsiveness, instructions, and transfer to the responsible person or adult required for that depth.
- An anesthesia-related incident causing injury or death and requiring hospitalization triggers a written Board report within thirty days under the compiled rule.
- A concurrent death must be reported within two working days, followed by the written report within thirty days.
- A failed facility inspection can require stopping sedation or anesthesia until the deficiencies are corrected and accepted.

The template is not a permit checklist and cannot establish clinical readiness.

## Hygienists and assistants

- Match every delegated act to the current license, certification, supervision level, and patient context in Rules 0460-03 and 0460-04.
- A Tennessee dental hygienist certified for local anesthesia may administer it only under the direct supervision described in Rule 0460-03-.12.
- After a hygienist administers local anesthesia, the record must state the date and time, administrator identity, anesthesia type, dose or amount, administration site, and any adverse reaction.
- Dental assistants may not administer local anesthesia, nitrous oxide, conscious sedation, or general anesthesia. A properly certified assistant may monitor nitrous oxide under the stated direct supervision and rule conditions.
- Exposure of dental radiographs by an assistant requires the certification and supervision described in Rule 0460-04-.11.
- General supervision does not authorize an act that otherwise requires direct supervision.

## Nitrous oxide

- A licensed dentist or properly certified dental hygienist under direct dentist supervision may administer nitrous oxide under Rule 0460-02-.07.
- The administering or supervising dentist must remain on the premises while nitrous oxide is in use.
- An authorized person must constantly monitor the patient by continuous direct clinical observation and notify the dentist of a possible adverse change.
- The delivery equipment must be able to provide at least 30 percent oxygen and must include a scavenging system.

## Infection control

Rule 0460-01-.11 requires, among other controls:

- approved instrument sterilization and cleaning between patients
- biological monitoring of heat sterilizers, immediate follow-up of a positive test, and removal from service after a second positive until repaired
- retention of sterilizer testing documentation in the dental office for at least two years
- EPA-registered environmental disinfectants used as directed, barriers changed between patients, and no reuse of single-use items
- required personal protective equipment, hand hygiene, sharps and bloodborne-pathogen controls, regulated-waste handling, and ventilation devices for resuscitation
- disinfection and biohazard handling of laboratory items and appliances

Use current CDC, OSHA, EPA, manufacturer, state, and local requirements when they are newer or stricter.

## Teledentistry

Rule 0460-01-.19 requires Tennessee licensure for dentistry on a patient in Tennessee and preserves ordinary supervision limits.

- The rule requires secure video conferencing or store-and-forward technology; audio-only, email or messaging alone, and fax alone do not meet its teledentistry definition.
- If transmitted information is inadequate, document that an opinion cannot be formed and obtain more data or direct examination or referral.
- Keep secure records with the same content required by Rule 0460-02-.12.
- Document teledentistry consent, the technology used, transmitted information, encounter facts, and limitations.
- For a patient younger than eighteen, apply the rule's parent or guardian presence requirement and any current statutory exception.

## Pediatric and special-needs restraint

Rule 0460-01-.18 contains training, assessment, consent, method, duration, documentation, parent-access, and least-restrictive requirements. It includes older terminology and a method that may conflict with newer professional guidance or facility policy.

- Do not treat the rule text as a recommendation to use restraint.
- Before any use, obtain current pediatric-dentistry guidance, legal review, credential verification, and facility approval.
- If used lawfully, the record must include the written consent required by the rule, exact method, reason, duration, type of restraint or immobilization, alternatives considered, monitoring, patient response, and parent-access event.

## Prescribing, controlled substances, and advertising

- Keep prescribing within the lawful practice of dentistry and document every prescribed or dispensed drug.
- Apply current Tennessee controlled-substance, electronic-prescribing, prescription-monitoring, pain-clinic, naloxone, and opioid-duration laws in addition to Rule 0460-02-.15. Do not rely on this summary to select a drug or quantity.
- Rule 0460-02-.15 sets minimum discipline after specified opioid-prescribing violations; it is not a prescribing protocol.
- Rule 0460-02-.10 prohibits false or misleading dental advertising, regulates advertised fees and specialty claims, assigns responsibility to licensees, and requires retention of advertising copies or recordings for two years.
