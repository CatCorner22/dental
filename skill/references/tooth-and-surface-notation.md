# U.S. tooth and surface notation

## Universal tooth designations

### Permanent dentition

- 1: maxillary right third molar
- 2 through 15: proceed across the maxillary arch
- 16: maxillary left third molar
- 17: mandibular left third molar
- 18 through 31: proceed across the mandibular arch
- 32: mandibular right third molar
- Supernumerary permanent teeth: 51 through 82, following the ADA Universal/National Tooth Designation System.

### Primary dentition

- A: maxillary right second molar
- B through I: proceed across the maxillary arch
- J: maxillary left second molar
- K: mandibular left second molar
- L through S: proceed across the mandibular arch
- T: mandibular right second molar
- Supernumerary primary teeth: add S to the adjacent primary designation, such as AS through TS, under the ADA system.

Do not use a number for a primary tooth or a letter for a permanent tooth. Document retained primary, unerupted permanent, congenitally missing, or supernumerary status separately.

## Official surface codes

| Code | Surface |
|---|---|
| B | buccal |
| D | distal |
| F | facial or labial |
| I | incisal |
| L | lingual |
| M | mesial |
| O | occlusal |

- Join multiple surfaces without spaces when a payer or EDR requires a compact code, such as MOD.
- Preserve the clinician's intended order when it has local meaning.
- Do not use P for palatal in an ADA claim surface field. Use L unless the EDR has a separate, defined anatomical field.

## Anatomy checks

- Permanent anterior teeth: 6 through 11 and 22 through 27.
- Primary anterior teeth: C through H and M through R.
- Anterior teeth ordinarily use I, not O.
- Posterior teeth ordinarily use O, not I.
- Buccal and facial can overlap in ordinary speech. Use the exact surface required by the clinical or claim context.
- Lingual may describe the palatal-facing surface in a claim field. Preserve palatal as an anatomical word in narrative when it adds precision.

Flag an unusual surface-tooth pairing. Do not automatically change it; the source record may describe a nonstandard lesion, restoration, or local field.

## Location hierarchy

Record the smallest supported unit:

1. oral or maxillofacial region
2. right or left side
3. maxillary or mandibular arch
4. quadrant or sextant
5. tooth or edentulous site
6. surface, root, canal, pocket, lesion, or implant component

Do not infer a tooth from a quadrant or infer a surface from a procedure.

## Cross-checks

- Verify the tooth exists in the stated dentition and eruption context.
- Verify side and arch against the Universal designation.
- Verify that image, diagnosis, consent, treatment plan, procedure note, prescription, laboratory order, and claim use the same site.
- Separate implant site from natural-tooth designation when the tooth is absent.
- Use range wording only when every included tooth is intended. Otherwise list each tooth.
- Do not use an en dash or hyphen if it could imply either a range or two separate sites.

## Source basis

Use the ADA Universal/National Tooth Designation System and the ADA claim-form surface values. Verify current payer and EDR requirements before operational use.
