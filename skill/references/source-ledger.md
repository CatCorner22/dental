# Template benchmark summary

## Conclusion

U.S. dental organizations generally publish record-content guidance, checklists, assessment forms, and parameters of care rather than complete paste-ready notes. Public EDR and vendor templates show useful workflow mechanics, but their clinical defaults are not authority.

Use this structure:

1. Universal Core
2. one or more procedure-site modules
3. high-risk overlay
4. state, facility, and practice overlay
5. deterministic and semantic audit
6. clinician comparison, EDR completion, signature, lock, and addendum control

## Adopted controls

- short ordered prompts
- no default diagnoses, findings, doses, outcomes, or no-complication statements
- procedure and site linkage
- exact care status
- visible unresolved placeholders
- required, conditional, EDR-only, optional, and not-applicable states
- deterministic plus semantic audit
- separate time-oriented anesthesia record
- readiness block while STOP or REQUIRED remains
- signed-entry lock and dated addendum in the EDR

The template set remains extensible. The Universal Procedure and Operative modules cover an uncommon technique only when paired with the practice's approved procedure-specific checklist.

## Research method

- Prioritized U.S. government sources, national dental and medical organizations, current guidelines, PubMed, and U.S.-published peer-reviewed journals.
- Queried Google Scholar as a discovery channel. Direct Scholar result pages were not accessible in the research environment. Verified included scholarly claims through PubMed, journal pages, institutional repositories, or association publications.
- Used abstracts only where full text was unavailable and labeled that limit.
- Checked pivotal PubMed records and targeted correction or retraction searches. No correction or retraction was identified for the pivotal documentation studies as of the review date. This is not a guarantee of future status.
- Did not reproduce licensed CDT or SNODENT code sets or copyrighted specialty manuals.

## Limits and implementation cautions

- U.S. state laws, board rules, retention rules, sedation permits, staffing, drug rules, and consent requirements vary. A state-specific legal and clinical review is required.
- Guidelines do not replace clinical judgment or a facility's emergency plan.
- The phrase all procedures is open-ended. The template set covers standard general, specialty, hospital-linked, imaging, and anesthesia domains and includes a universal operative module for uncommon procedures.
- The Skill cannot certify HIPAA de-identification, documentation completeness, code correctness, standard of care, or billing compliance.
- Books and proprietary standards were used only as user-supplied style direction or source pointers. No protected wording or code-set content is reproduced.
