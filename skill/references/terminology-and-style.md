# Controlled terminology and style

## Core rules

- Preserve clinical meaning over reading level.
- Use active voice, direct subjects, and past tense for completed acts.
- Keep sentences short. Put one action or finding in each sentence.
- Use one term for one concept.
- Use exact site, tooth, surface, side, quantity, unit, route, and time when supplied.
- Do not use and/or, etc., a slash to express alternatives, or a question mark to express uncertainty.
- Avoid respectively and ambiguous pronouns. Repeat the tooth, site, drug, device, or actor when needed.
- Do not turn a blank into normal, negative, absent, or not applicable.
- Do not write judgmental labels. Record the observed act, the stated choice, and the clinical effect.

## Status values

### Evidence source

- Clinician observed
- Patient reports
- Parent or guardian reports
- External record states
- Test result shows
- Image interpretation states

### Data state

- Present
- Absent
- Not assessed
- Not applicable
- Unknown
- Unresolved

### Procedure state

- Recommended
- Planned
- Authorized
- Consented
- Started
- Completed
- Partly completed
- Stopped
- Deferred
- Declined

Do not collapse these states.

## Replace vague or unsafe phrases

| Avoid | Write instead |
|---|---|
| tolerated well | state observed response, complications, vital-status facts, and condition at transfer |
| hemostasis achieved | state method and observed bleeding status |
| consent obtained | state the discussion topics, questions, decision, and form status |
| all questions answered | the clinician answered the patient's stated questions |
| normal | name the structure, test, range, or finding |
| stable | name the unchanged finding, comparison, and period |
| adequate | state the criterion that was met |
| significant | state the measurement or clinical effect |
| minimal, moderate, or large | give a measurement or defined scale; keep formal anesthesia-depth terms |
| routine | name the reason, interval, or procedure |
| as needed | state the trigger and action |
| appears | state the observation and reason for uncertainty |
| noncompliant | state the missed action or declined care and its clinical effect |
| patient denies | patient reports no, followed by the exact symptom or event |
| poor historian | identify the missing fact and source limitation |
| unremarkable | name the structure and observed finding |
| grossly intact | name the tested function and result |
| no complications | no complication was observed during the stated procedure or observation period |

## Clinical uncertainty

Use:

- Working diagnosis: <clinician-supplied term>.
- Evidence supporting it: <fact>.
- Evidence against or not yet known: <fact>.
- Next diagnostic step: <clinician-supplied plan>.

Do not turn a differential diagnosis into a final diagnosis.

## Medication language

Record only supplied facts:

- generic name
- concentration
- amount and unit
- total dose and unit
- route
- administration time
- indication
- prescriber or administrator role
- response or adverse event

Do not convert between cartridges, milliliters, milligrams, or micrograms unless the clinician has verified the calculation in the source record.

## Plain-language instructions

- Put the action first.
- Use common words and short sentences.
- Define necessary clinical terms once.
- State the exact trigger for calling the office or seeking urgent care.
- Do not weaken the clinical meaning to reach a target grade level.
- Aim for about fifth-grade language for patient-facing text when possible; keep clinical notes technically precise.

## Adams-style ambiguity controls

- Give each defined term one meaning.
- Use must for a required workflow action, may for permission, and will for a future fact. Avoid shall.
- Avoid lists that mix actions, conditions, and exceptions.
- State conditions with if. State exceptions with except.
- State who acts. Do not use passive voice when the actor matters.
- Keep each condition next to the action it controls.
- Use a separate sentence for each procedure-site-status combination.
- Do not quote or imitate proprietary wording from any drafting manual.
